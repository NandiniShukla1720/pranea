Yoga for Every Phase of Your Cycle

By Nandini Shukla·5 min read·Movement Science

Most fitness advice is built around a single, flat assumption: that your body has roughly the same capacity every day of the month. If you menstruate, you already know that's not quite true. Energy, joint laxity, pain tolerance and motivation all shift across the cycle — and yoga, more than most forms of movement, is flexible enough to shift with you.

Menstrual phase: grounding over intensity

Estrogen and progesterone are both at their monthly low here, and many people experience cramping caused by prostaglandins — hormone-like compounds that trigger uterine contractions. Restorative poses like Balasana (Child's Pose) and Supta Baddha Konasana gently release tension in the lower back and hips without demanding much from an already-tired body. This is a legitimate rest phase, not a phase to push through.

Follicular phase: building strength and heat

As estrogen begins rising, most people notice a genuine uptick in energy and mood. This is usually the best window to build strength and stamina — poses like Bhujangasana (Cobra), Trikonasana (Triangle), and a light Sun Salutation flow take advantage of that rising capacity. It's also a good window to try a new, slightly more demanding pose, since joints and connective tissue tend to feel more resilient here.

Ovulatory phase: the peak, briefly

Around ovulation, estrogen hits its monthly high and many people report feeling their strongest and most coordinated. It's worth noting, though, that peak estrogen is also associated with slightly looser ligaments — so this is a good window for strength and balance work, but not necessarily for pushing the deepest possible stretch. Poses like Malasana (Garland Pose) support pelvic floor and hip mobility without overextending.

Luteal phase: steady and internal

As progesterone rises after ovulation, energy tends to dip again, particularly in the final week before your period. This is where I'd reach for Setu Bandhasana (Bridge) for a gentle mood lift, Paschimottanasana (Seated Forward Fold) for its naturally calming, introspective quality, and Viparita Karani (Legs-Up-The-Wall) to ease the fluid retention that often shows up here.

The goal was never to do the hardest version of every pose, every day. It was to have the right pose for the day you're actually having.

A note on consistency

What I've found most useful isn't any single pose — it's the habit of checking in with where I am in my cycle before deciding what kind of movement makes sense that day. Some poses, like Marjaryasana-Bitilasana (Cat-Cow) and a short Shavasana, work well in every single phase, which makes them a reliable daily anchor even when everything else is shifting.
