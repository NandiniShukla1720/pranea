Strength & Recovery

Why lifting matters for you, specifically.

Strength training isn't just aesthetic — for women, it's directly tied to bone density, metabolic health and hormonal resilience.

💪 Strength Basics — Why It Matters & A Sample Routine+

Why it matters for women: Estrogen plays a protective role in bone density — resistance training builds bone mass while estrogen is available, creating a buffer for the years ahead. It also improves insulin sensitivity, which can ease PCOS-related symptoms, and builds the muscular support your pelvic floor and lower back rely on through every phase.

15-Minute Beginner Circuit · 2–3x / week

  • Bodyweight Squats — 3 sets × 12 reps — builds lower-body and pelvic-floor strength
  • Wall or Knee Push-Ups — 3 sets × 8–10 reps — upper-body and core strength
  • Glute Bridges — 3 sets × 15 reps — strengthens glutes and lower back
  • Standing Resistance-Band Rows — 3 sets × 12 reps — supports posture
  • Dead Bug — 3 sets × 10 reps/side — deep core stability

Progressing further (intermediate): Add light dumbbells to squats and rows, introduce a Romanian deadlift for the hamstrings and glutes, and build up to a full push-up. Aim to increase reps or resistance every 2–3 weeks, not every session.

Best done in the follicular or ovulatory phase, when energy and strength typically peak — you can still train in luteal or menstrual weeks, just expect to lift a little lighter.

🌿 Recovery & Mobility — Why It Matters & A Sample Routine+

Why it matters for women: Recovery isn't laziness — it's when muscle actually rebuilds and your nervous system resets. This matters even more across a cycle, since the luteal and menstrual phases naturally lower your tolerance for high-intensity training. Respecting that rhythm reduces injury risk and burnout.

10-Minute Mobility Reset · Daily or as needed

  • Neck & Shoulder Rolls — 1 minute — releases desk-day tension
  • Cat-Cow Flow — 8 rounds — mobilises the spine, eases cramps
  • Seated Forward Fold — hold 60 seconds — calms the nervous system
  • Figure-4 Hip Stretch — 45 seconds/side — releases hip and glute tightness
  • Legs-Up-the-Wall — 5 minutes — reduces bloating and lower-body swelling

Signs you need a recovery day: unusually sore muscles beyond 48 hours, poor sleep, irritability, or a resting heart rate noticeably higher than usual. All are signals to swap strength training for mobility work.

Ideal during menstrual or luteal phases, or any low-energy day — this is maintenance, not a step backward.
